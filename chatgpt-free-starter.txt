Shortlist English-Taught Masters in Germany

Help me find fully English-taught master's programs I qualify for at public higher education institutions in Germany. Research in English and deliver a downloadable Excel workbook. Follow these steps.

1. Collect my profile

Ask one short question at a time and wait for my answer. Use the checklist below privately and skip anything I already provided. In Work, use one interactive prompt when available. Offer a few clear choices when the answers are predictable, such as Winter, Summer or Either for the intake. Let me type a custom answer. For an open answer such as a degree subject or grade, use a free-text prompt if available or ask one question in chat. Use the same one-question flow in regular chat. If I say I don't know or want to skip, continue with what can be checked and leave any affected eligibility decision unresolved.

Collect these details as needed:
- Bachelor's subject and degree title, university and awarding country, completion date or expected graduation, overall grade, and grading scale with the best grade and lowest passing grade.
- Ask what courses and credits I can share, even if I have only some or none. If available, collect completion status, the credit system and relevant grades. A redacted transcript or course handbook is optional. Ask for a specific missing fact or short description only when it could change a decision. Tell me to remove personal identifiers from any file.
- Target intake and academic year, preferred master's fields or tracks, and any preferred German states or cities. Search nationwide by default.
- Ask one optional question about city fit. Offer examples such as climate, jobs or internships in my field, access to nature, city size, and cost of living. Let me skip, list my priorities, or identify a must-have. Separate preferences from hard filters.
- Tuition budget, currency and period (semester, year or degree), whether the budget is a hard cap, and whether compulsory fees are included. Ask for any applicant fee category and English test, score, date, or exemption that could affect the results.

2. Check official sources one program at a time

Use this directory of public German institutions as the discovery queue:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akifnu/shortlist-english-masters/main/shortlist-english-masters/assets/german-public-universities.csv
Open the CSV or ask me to attach it if you cannot retrieve it. Read only a small batch of institutions at a time. Begin with official university catalogs, and cross-check candidate discovery with DAAD International Programmes and Hochschulkompass. Open each candidate's official program site and follow the university's links. Read all applicable admissions provisions, including governing ordinances, appendices, amendments, application guides, FAQs and footnotes that affect eligibility. Check effective dates and the target intake. Use the current governing text when an older page or a translation disagrees. Read the original language where needed and report the facts in English. Use narrow page ranges for long PDFs.

Confirm an entirely English-taught path through compulsory modules, assessments, placements and the thesis. Confirm total and subject-specific credits, required courses and grade thresholds, minimum degree duration or equivalence, language proof and exceptions, and any other mandatory prerequisite. Map my completed modules to exact credit categories without double-counting. Apply only grading and credit conversion rules accepted by the university. Confirm that the intake and deadlines apply to me. Open the actual application route and verify the relevant tuition, semester contributions, and other mandatory fees. Treat ordinary application steps and competitive ranking separately from academic eligibility.

Keep a program out if a required qualification, the English-taught path, a hard filter, or a material application or cost detail remains unverified. Ask me for specific missing evidence when it could change a decision. If a controlling document is inaccessible, ask me for the official PDF or page and keep checking independent candidates. Cite each rule or fact with an official link and page, table or section. Use third-party program listings only for discovery.

Start research with whatever academic details I can provide. Use a clear self-reported course list and label its origin. A known subset can satisfy a minimum without a full transcript. Use an official module catalog for content or credit clarification where it matches my program and cohort. Confirm course completion from what I report or supply. Follow any document requirement at application. If a mandatory prerequisite remains unverified, keep it out of my confirmed shortlist and summarize the missing facts anonymously.

For campus city comparisons, verify the factors I named using relevant sources such as local labor-market data, official climate normals, municipal park or transport information, or published housing figures. Treat my city priorities as ranking preferences unless I explicitly mark a must-have. Cite the factual basis, explain trade-offs, and identify any missing city data. Keep an explicit must-have unverified if the evidence is insufficient.

3. Save progress in small batches

After each batch of about five programs, save a compact checkpoint if file tools are available. Otherwise give me a short copyable checkpoint when needed. Include the search scope, relevant academic facts without personal identifiers, the last directory batch and any state filter, the next candidate to review, decisions, and compact source citations. Keep private working notes separate from the workbook. Resume from the most recent checkpoint if I provide one, and avoid rereading sources that are already verified. Keep going until the agreed scope is covered. If a limit interrupts the search, label the results partial and tell me what remains.

4. Deliver the workbook

Create and check one .xlsx with four tabs:
- Shortlist: only verified eligible programs. Include university, master's program or track, campus and country, intake semester and year, application window and deadline, tuition, semester contribution, other mandatory fees, official program and application links, official admissions rules, any key application steps, and exactly 150 words describing how my studies align with the program's curriculum.
- In every Shortlist row, include a separate written Ordinance compliance assessment. Identify the controlling ordinance or amendment for my intake. Cite the sections, paragraphs or annexes in the prose. Compare my grade, total credits, language proof and other applicable qualifications with the published thresholds. Name the completed modules and ECTS used to meet each subject-credit minimum. Explain any applicable exception and any remaining university selection or verification step.
- If I gave city preferences, rank the eligible Shortlist rows by my priorities and add a brief City fit note. Use the actual teaching campus.
- Admissions Evidence: one row per relevant eligibility rule, city finding or key fact, my evidence or the verified finding, and the official source link and section or page. Cite the curriculum used for the fit description.
- Documents Reviewed: official links, applicable versions, and sections or page ranges read for the shortlisted programs.
- Search Summary: research date, geographic and program scope, coverage limits, whether the search is complete, and counts for all outcomes. Add my city priorities and anonymized academic evidence gaps when applicable. Omit excluded and unverified program names throughout the workbook.

Check that required fields and links are present, each academic fit paragraph is 150 words, each shortlisted program has a written ordinance compliance assessment, and any city ranking and notes match my priorities. Return the workbook even if the completed search produced no confirmed matches. If file creation is unavailable, give me the same four tables in copyable form and explain the file limitation.

Start with one short question for the next missing detail. If you have enough information already, begin the research.
